# MB Blog Voice Reference (long-form)

Built from ~140 real Maurice Blackburn blog articles, including every one of the 100 most-recently-modified posts (verified via the sitemap `lastmod`, current to 2 August 2026) plus a spread of older evergreen posts, across medical-injury, road-injury, workplace-injury, workplace-disease-exposure, superannuation, asbestos/dust-diseases, wills-estates, class-actions, public-place-injury, childhood-abuse, employment-issues, financial-advice-disputes and consumer-protection. Every quote below is lifted verbatim from a named live blog. Use this as the mandatory voice pass for any long-form MB content: match these patterns, kill the off-brand tells, run the rubric before shipping.

The core voice below is stable across the whole archive. The newest posts (2025-2026) add a few structural habits worth matching: a visible "Last updated" date, an optional "In summary" summary block up top, a standard FAQ section at the foot (question headings), and stats cited with a year/source. Those are captured in the Headings + structure section. **Before matching voice, identify which of the six content genres you are writing (next section): each has its own opener, shape and CTA.**

Australian English throughout. No em dashes (MB house style: use commas, colons, or full stops).

## Voice in one paragraph

MB long-form reads like a knowledgeable friend who happens to be a lawyer: warm, plain-English, calm, and squarely on the reader's side. It opens by giving the reader something useful straight away (a direct answer, a clear stat, or a real person's story), never by throat-clearing about "today's fast-paced world". Sentences are short to medium and mostly one idea each; paragraphs are tight. It handles trauma (cancer, abuse, fatal accidents) with genuine empathy and restraint, and switches to crisp, procedural clarity for mechanics like time limits and scheme rules. It never guarantees outcomes: entitlement is always hedged ("you may be entitled", "you may be eligible", "you may have a claim"). Clients are named real people (Carol, Neil, Ann, Amanda), never "the plaintiff". It closes by pointing gently to help ("We are here to help", "You are not alone", Free Claim Check, an obligation-free consult), advocacy not sales.

## DO patterns (each anchored to a real blog)

### Openers: lead with the answer, the stat, or the person, never with filler

- **Direct answer to the title question.** Discharge against medical advice: "Discharging yourself from hospital against medical advice can result in serious consequences, like making your condition worse, or in some cases, death."
- **Direct answer, then steps.** Road accident in Victoria: "Knowing what to do after a car accident in Victoria is important. If you've been involved in a road accident, the first four steps you should take are:"
- **A concrete stat that matters to the reader.** Bowel cancer: "Every year, more than 5,600 Australians die from bowel cancer, that's around 100 people each week."
- **A real person, named, in an ordinary moment.** Carol's story: "In January 2017, Carol noticed an unexplained lump on her right eyelid and went to her doctor for a check-up." Neil's story: "For over 45 years, Neil worked as a plumber."
- **Direct address to the reader ("you").** Motorcyclist safety: "Whether you're on a long ride enjoying the scenery and sunshine, or you're filtering through bumper to bumper traffic to get to work in the morning, you deserve to be kept safe on our roads."
- **A plain diagnostic question that surfaces the reader's situation.** WorkCover Victoria: "Have you suffered an injury at work or been diagnosed with a work-related illness? Did you know you may be entitled to make a claim for compensation?"
- **A cited, current stat (newer posts).** High blood pressure/stroke: "High blood pressure is the leading modifiable risk factor for stroke, yet many Australians remain unaware they have it." (body cites "Up to 80% of strokes could be prevented" and "In 2023-24, more than 18,300 serious workers' compensation claims").
- **A news or policy hook, then the systemic point (newer opinion-led posts).** Volunteer firefighters: "The recent ABC report into the Nar Nar Goon CFA brigade has highlighted the devastating impact occupational cancer can have on firefighters and their families."

### Empathy on heavy topics: name the human cost first, plainly

- Triple-negative breast cancer: "Being told you have cancer is something no one wants to hear. Being told that you have a rare and aggressive form of cancer is even more devastating, especially when you're in the prime of your life."
- Coronial process: "The death of a loved one in any circumstances can be extremely traumatic for family and friends. But when there are unanswered questions about what or why a death happened, it can make that grief even harder to bear."
- Abuse (support): "the most valuable support you can provide is to listen"; the team is described as "trauma-informed".
- Abuse (advocacy): closes on "Our team of experienced abuse lawyers are here to help. You are not alone."

### Hedged entitlement language: real help, never a guarantee

MB never promises a win or a payout. The recurring frames:
- "you may be entitled to compensation" (medical negligence articles, repeated)
- "you may be eligible for compensation" (public event injury)
- "you may still be able to make a claim for compensation" (waivers)
- "you may have a claim for medical negligence" (discharge against advice)
- "you should be be eligible for workers' compensation" (WFH injuries) [note: hedged with "should", conditions listed first]
- When "No Win, No Fee" appears it is stated factually, not as a hook: "Our No Win, No Fee policy means that if we don't win, you don't have to pay our legal fees."

### CTA style: advocacy, warmth, a clear next step (often the Free Claim Check)

- Road/employment: "We are here to help. Give us a call, request a call back or use our free claim check tool to get in touch with our friendly legal team."
- Workplace PTSD: "Contact us today for an obligation-free first consultation or use our free claim check tool to find out if you may have a claim."
- Coronial: "Get in touch with our experienced medical negligence lawyers who can provide support and guidance throughout the process."
- Abuse: "If you or someone you love is a survivor of childhood abuse, we are here to support you and get the justice you deserve." / "You are not alone." / "We will listen and believe you."
- Wills (Ann's story), closing on the client's own words: "Get an estate plan in order. I can't stress this enough. Just do it today."
- Newer posts standardise a phone-plus-tool close: "Call 1800 111 222 to speak to us sooner or complete our Free Claim Check online." / "Use our free online claim check tool, send us a message or call us on 1800 111 222." The closing subheading is very often "We're here to help".

### Heading style: body headings are statements or plain reader-questions; FAQ headings are questions

- **Statement / imperative body headings:** "Carol's life changed forever"; "Time limits apply to medical negligence claims"; "Don't put off planning for the future"; "Workplace injuries can be psychological"; "Strength in numbers".
- **Plain reader-voice questions used as navigational headings** (how-to and scheme mechanics): "What is medical negligence?"; "How do you know if you have a work injury severe enough to make a WorkCover claim?"; "Am I eligible for compensation from the Mackay hospital?"; "Who is legally responsible for safety at events in Australia?"
- **Numbered how-to headings** for practical guides: "1. Assume car drivers can't see you", "2. Watch out for road works" (motorcyclist safety); "See your own GP", "Keep your own records of workplace accidents" (work accident steps).

## DON'T / off-brand tells (contrasted)

- **Generic SEO throat-clearing.** Generic: "In today's fast-paced world, accidents can happen to anyone." MB: opens with the actual answer or a real stat ("Every year, more than 5,600 Australians die from bowel cancer").
- **Corporate self-praise.** Generic: "Our team of dedicated professionals is committed to excellence." MB: names the practical service and stays reader-first: "Our experienced team specialise in medical negligence compensation claims and are dedicated to protecting the rights and entitlements of you and your family." (Note: MB does say "experienced/expert team", but always tied to what it does for the reader, never as standalone puffery.)
- **Outcome guarantees / hype.** Never: "We will win you the maximum compensation you deserve." MB always hedges: "you may be entitled to compensation".
- **Hedge-stacking.** Don't pile qualifiers: "you might possibly potentially be able to perhaps claim." One clean hedge: "you may be entitled to compensation."
- **"The plaintiff" / "the claimant" / "the victim" as the main referent.** MB uses real first names: Carol, Neil, Ann, Amanda, Lauren, Kyle, Paul, Ben, Bronwyn. (It uses "survivor", "worker", "patient" as respectful category nouns, not clinical distancing.)
- **Fear-mongering.** Heavy facts are stated plainly and paired with a way forward ("with the right guidance, you can make it through the process"), never used to scare the reader into calling.
- **Listicle blandness / keyword stuffing.** Numbered lists exist but each item is a real, specific action ("Assume car drivers can't see you"), not padded keyword filler.
- **Em dashes.** MB house style: use commas, colons or full stops instead.

## Sensitive-topic handling (trauma/grief/abuse vs procedural)

**On trauma, grief, cancer, abuse, fatal accidents:**
- Lead with the human reality, gently and plainly ("The death of a loved one in any circumstances can be extremely traumatic"). No stats-first cold open on the most personal topics.
- Slow the pace. Short sentences, space to breathe. Acknowledge the reader's feelings before any legal content.
- Reassure and reduce isolation: "You are not alone"; "we are here to support you"; describe the team as "trauma-informed".
- Position compensation honestly, as support not a prize: "While compensation cannot reverse the harm that has been done, it can provide financial support for medical expenses, lost income, and other damages caused."
- Where a real person is featured (Carol, Neil, Amanda, Ann), tell their story with dignity and let their own words carry the close.

**On procedural / scheme mechanics (time limits, TAC/WorkCover, eligibility, redress options):**
- Switch to crisp, confident, organised prose. Clear reader-question headings, numbered steps, plain definitions ("A class action is a lawsuit that allows the victims of wrongs to group together...").
- Still reader-first and hedged on entitlement, but the empathy dials down and the clarity dials up. State facts, options and next steps without emotional framing.
- Flag deadlines helpfully, not fearfully: "There are time limits, so find out where you stand as soon as possible after your accident or diagnosis."

## Heading + structure conventions

- **Answer-first opening sentence.** The first sentence earns its place: it answers the title question, gives a real stat, or introduces a named person. No warm-up.
- **Statement headings for the body**, reserved plain questions for genuine reader FAQs and scheme mechanics. A how-to guide can use numbered imperative headings.
- **One idea per paragraph.** Keep paragraphs tight; let the page breathe, especially on sensitive topics.
- **Named human stories** are a signature structural device: either the whole article is one person's story (Carol, Neil, Ann, Amanda, Angie) or a case study sits mid-article as supporting evidence (Bronwyn, Lauren, Kyle, Paul, Ben). Story-led posts use evocative statement headings ("A mistake that should never have happened", "A safe place to land", "Looking forward").
- **Close with a warm, specific CTA** that offers a real next step: Free Claim Check, obligation-free consult, "get in touch", or the phone number 1800 111 222, framed as help/advocacy ("We're here to help", "You are not alone"), never a hard sell.

### Newer-post structural habits (2025-2026), match these on fresh content
- **Visible "Last updated" date** on the article. Fresh content should read as current and dated.
- **Optional "In summary" block** near the top for guide-style posts (e.g. the TAC claim lawyer guide), giving the answer up front before the detail.
- **A standard FAQ section at the foot**, with genuine reader-question headings ("Do I need a lawyer to make a TAC claim?", "What does 'No Win, No Fee' mean?", "What if my claim has been denied?"). This is where questions belong; keep body headings as statements.
- **Stats cited with a year or source**, not floated vaguely: "In 2023-24, more than 18,300 serious workers' compensation claims", "Up to 80% of strokes could be prevented". If you cite a number, ground it.
- **Class-action / settlement posts use an announcement tone**, not the empathetic explainer voice: factual, precise on amounts and eligibility windows ("acquired shares in Crown between 11 December 2014 and 18 October 2020"), and they link out to the relevant class-action page rather than to a claim check. Match register to purpose.

## Content genres (identify the type first, each has its own template)

MB blog posts are not one shape. Across the current top-100 they fall into six recognisable genres. Pick the genre before drafting; it decides the opener, the structure and the CTA. Practice-area vocabulary (next section) layers on top.

1. **Evergreen explainer / guide.** The workhorse. Answers a question or explains a scheme. Opener = direct answer or diagnostic question ("Total and permanent disability is assessed based on work capacity, not diagnosis alone."). Structure = reader-question subheadings, optional "In summary" block, FAQ at the foot. CTA = Free Claim Check / consult for that area. Recurring heading formula: "What is X?", "How to X", "Do you have a claim?", "What compensation could you receive?".

2. **Client story.** One named person's journey, a huge and growing genre (Ron, Matthew, Teresa, Vicky, Tracey, Tom, Brian, Wendy, Steve, Bert, Joshua, John, Kerry, Tori, Mark). Opener = a vivid first-person quote or an ordinary-life scene: "I've lost everything," says Vicky. / "My dad would come home from work covered in asbestos." / "Mining was more than just a job to him; it was all he knew." Structure = narrative statement headings ("A lifetime of hard work cut short", "The injury that changed everything", "Ready to start a new chapter"), the MB lawyer named, the client's dignity kept, their own words at the close. CTA = warm, area-appropriate.

3. **News-hook / incident explainer.** Anchored to a named hospital, place or report in the news (Buderim, Northern Beaches, Toowoomba, Joondalup, corridor care, Heatherton Road, Albert Park asbestos, Venture Egg). Opener = the report/incident: "Recent reports about care concerns at Buderim Private Hospital have raised questions about patient safety...". Structure = "What we know so far" → "Who is actually responsible for your care?" → "When does poor care become medical negligence in [STATE]?" → contact the relevant team. Measured and factual, not alarmist. Cites the inquiry/coroner/regulator by name (HaDSCO, Health Care Complaints Commission, Clinical Excellence Commission).

4. **Awareness day / week hook.** Tied to a calendar moment (National Stroke Week, Birth Trauma Awareness Week, World Asthma Day, Make March Purple, International Women's Day, Thank a First Responder Day, Mesothelioma Awareness Day). Opener = the moment + why it matters: "13-19 July 2026 marks Birth Trauma Awareness Week...". Structure = educate first (signs, risks), then "When care falls short" → legal options. CTA is genuinely advocacy: sometimes it links OUT to the cause itself ("Find out how you can Make March Purple this month"), not to MB, which is a real brand tell, MB earns trust by not always selling.

5. **Class-action announcement / precedent.** Never the empathetic voice, always factual and authoritative. Three sub-types: (a) settlement approved, cite the section (s 33V Supreme Court Act 1986 (Vic)), the Justice, the dollar amount, the registered group-member count, "fair and reasonable"; (b) procedural/precedent ruling, GCO granted, carriage decided, class closure refused, trial vacated, cite the Act and Justice and explain the significance; (c) thought-leadership editorial ("By any measure, 2025 was a landmark year...", bylined by the National Head of Class Actions). CTA = link to the specific class-action / settlement-payments page or /class-actions/, NEVER a Free Claim Check.

6. **Open class-action recruitment.** Distinct from the announcements: for a live action, opener = the scale of the wrong ("We believe more than one million Australians have suffered financial loss..."), headings like "Strength in numbers" / "How can you join?", tells the reader "you are automatically a group member" and how to register. CTA = register / dedicated email + phone.

## Per-practice-area language (each area has its own vocabulary and CTA model)

The core voice is constant, but the terminology, the scheme names, and even the call-to-action change by practice area. Using the wrong area's vocabulary is an instant tell. Match the area:

- **Road injury (VIC = TAC).** Scheme: Transport Accident Commission (TAC), a "no-fault" scheme. Terms: no-fault benefits, income support (up to 80% of pre-injury earnings), impairment benefit, common law claim (where another party was negligent), pain and suffering as non-economic loss, statutory thresholds/caps, CTP, lodge within 12 months (TAC discretion to 3 years). Disputes: No-Fault Dispute Resolution Protocols, VCAT. Uninsured-driver angle: letter of demand, wage garnishment. Pedestrian/road-toll pieces use "vulnerable road users". Opener: "A TAC claim is meant to support you, but getting the support you're entitled to isn't always simple." CTA: "free road injury claim check tool" + 1800 111 222 + No Win No Fee.
- **Workplace injury (WorkCover).** Terms: WorkCover, weekly payments / weekly income support, medical expenses, lump sum impairment benefit, common law claim, contributory negligence, dependency claim (for a workplace death). Entitlement frame: "you may be entitled to: weekly income support, medical expenses, lump sum impairment benefits." CTA: Free Claim Check + 1800 111 222.
- **Workplace disease / occupational exposure.** Terms: hazardous chemicals, cumulative exposure, latency period, occupational disease, duty of care, engineering controls, PPE, presumptive rights / "presumed entitlement" (firefighters), dependency claims. Often opens on a news hook or cited stat, then a systemic point.
- **Superannuation / insurance.** Terms: TPD (total and permanent disability), "own occupation" vs "any occupation" definitions, functional/vocational capacity assessment, income protection, "insurance attached to your super", preservation age, waiting period, date of disability, offsetting clauses, partial disability benefit, group vs retail policy, common rejection reasons (evidence gaps, permanence disputes). Signature reassurance: the injury "doesn't need to be work-related" and "can be physical or psychological". Doubly hedged: "you may be eligible to make a claim on your superannuation insurance." CTA: "free super claim check" (/free-claim-check/superannuation/) + 1800 196 050, not the generic Free Claim Check.
- **Asbestos / dust diseases.** Terms: mesothelioma, asbestosis, silicosis, progressive massive fibrosis, pleural thickening / pleural plaques, respirable crystalline silica, latency period, legacy asbestos, engineered stone ban (1 July 2024), Dust Diseases Tribunal, secondary and environmental exposure, duty of care. MB-specific asset: the free National Dust Register to document exposure early. Almost always human-story led (Neil, Brian, Wendy, Tom, Steve, Bert, Joshua) or news-hook (mulch crisis, Albert Park, illegal dumping). CTA: register exposure / Get in touch / call 1800 111 222 + No Win No Fee.
- **Medical injury / negligence.** Definitional anchor: "Medical negligence occurs when a health professional's treatment falls below the accepted standard of care, causing injury or harm to a patient." Terms: delayed / missed diagnosis, adverse outcome, standard of care, informed consent, failure to escalate, coronial inquest / coroner's recommendations, "time limits apply" (NSW Limitation Act 1969, generally 3 years), wrongful birth, placental abruption, birth trauma. Regulators cited by state: HaDSCO (WA), Health Care Complaints Commission (NSW), Clinical Excellence Commission. Very common as news-hook/incident explainers (named hospital + "When does poor care become medical negligence in [STATE]?"). Hedged: "you may be entitled to compensation." CTA: free initial consultation + No Win No Fee.
- **Public place / public liability.** Terms: duty of care, public liability claim, negligence, breach of duty, foreseeable risk, occupier, contributory negligence, pain and suffering. Aviation/event angle: Civil Aviation (Carriers' Liability) Regulations 2019 (up to $925,000), CASA, strict 2-year time limit. Signature move on fault: "Even if you were partly at fault, you may still be entitled to compensation, although it could be reduced." Relatable scene openers: "A trip to the shops shouldn't end in an ambulance ride." CTA: /free-claim-check/public-liability/ + Get in touch.
- **Wills & estates.** Two sub-voices. Planning: Will, executor, capacity, estate plan, advance care directive, valid signing/witnessing (interested witness rule), marriage can revoke a Will, digital assets / cryptocurrency private keys ("Just do it today"). Disputes/administration: intestate / intestacy, letters of administration, grant of probate, probate caveat, administrator, beneficiary, statutory formula, undue influence, contest / challenge a Will, family provision claim (Part IV), survivorship / joint ownership, strict 6-12 month time limits. CTA points to Will dispute lawyers or an estate-planning consult, not a claim check.
- **Class actions.** Terms: group member ("you are automatically a group member"), settlement, shareholder class action, continuous disclosure obligations, misleading or deceptive conduct, GCO (group costs order), litigation funding, register. Two registers: recruitment for open actions ("Strength in numbers", "How can you join?") and factual announcement for settlements (precise amounts and eligibility windows, "the Honourable Justice ... handed down a landmark judgment"). CTA links to the specific class-action page, not a claim check.
- **Childhood abuse.** Terms: survivor (never "victim"), trauma-informed, National Redress Scheme, civil damages claim, institutional abuse, re-traumatisation. Empathy-max, choice and pace emphasised: "You are not alone", "We will listen and believe you". Hedged: "you may be entitled to claim compensation."
- **Employment issues.** Terms: Fair Work Act, unfair dismissal, genuine redundancy, redundancy pay / notice periods, consultation, enterprise agreement, award, 21-day claim deadline, general protections, procedural fairness, restraint of trade / non-solicitation (executive contracts), workplace investigation, discrimination (Disability Discrimination Act 1992 (Cth)), sexual harassment (Respect@Work), "trauma-informed legal support". Distinct commercial model: CTA is a paid "Book a consultation" (fixed-fee general consult, /campaign/book-a-consultation/), NOT Free Claim Check or No Win No Fee. "It doesn't cost you anything to know where you stand."
- **Financial advice disputes.** Terms: AFCA (Australian Financial Complaints Authority), Compensation Scheme of Last Resort, AFSL, authorised representative, negligent financial advice, underinsurance, Corporations Act ongoing-review duty, personal insurance types (life, income protection, TPD, trauma), lodge a complaint. Often news-hook openers (a fund collapse or a Federal Court asset freeze). Hedged: "you may be eligible to lodge a complaint with AFCA." CTA: "Contact us today" / 1800 111 222.
- **Consumer protection.** Terms: Civil Aviation (Carriers' Liability) Act, travel insurance claim, delayed vs lost luggage, stated thresholds ($3,000 checked / $300 unchecked). Relatable-frustration openers.

**CTA model by area (get this right):** personal-injury areas close on a Free Claim Check + 1800 111 222 + No Win No Fee, and the claim-check is segmented by area (/free-claim-check/superannuation/, /free-claim-check/public-liability/, "free road injury claim check tool"); superannuation also uses 1800 196 050; employment uses a paid Book-a-consultation (/campaign/book-a-consultation/); class-action announcements link to the specific action / settlement-payments page (never a claim check); wills point to estate-planning or Will-dispute lawyers; awareness-day pieces sometimes link OUT to the cause itself, advocacy over sell.

## Voice pass rubric (run every check on a draft; any NO needs a fix)

1. **Answer-first?** Does the opening sentence give a direct answer, a real stat, or a named person, rather than generic throat-clearing?
2. **No SEO filler?** Zero "in today's fast-paced world", "when it comes to", "we all know that" padding?
3. **No corporate puffery?** Any "team of dedicated professionals"-style self-praise removed or tied to concrete reader benefit?
4. **Entitlement hedged?** Every claim of eligibility uses "may be entitled / may be eligible / may have a claim", with no guaranteed outcomes and no hedge-stacking?
5. **Clients named, not labelled?** Real first names used, not "the plaintiff / the claimant / the victim" as the main referent?
6. **Empathy calibrated to topic?** Trauma/grief content leads with the human cost and reassurance; procedural content is crisp and clear, not cold on the sensitive stuff or gushing on the mechanical stuff?
7. **Headings on-pattern?** Body headings are statements or plain reader-questions (questions genuinely reserved for FAQ/mechanics), not clickbait?
8. **One-idea paragraphs?** Short-to-medium sentences, tight paragraphs, room to breathe on heavy topics?
9. **CTA is advocacy, warm, specific?** Closes with a real next step (Free Claim Check, obligation-free consult, get in touch) framed as help, not a hard sell?
10. **Stats grounded?** Any statistic is cited with a year or source and reads as current, not a vague floated number?
11. **Right genre template?** Identified which of the six genres this is (evergreen explainer, client story, news-hook/incident, awareness-day, class-action announcement, open-action recruitment) and followed its opener/structure/CTA?
12. **Register matches purpose?** Explainer/story posts are warm and empathetic; class-action/settlement posts are factual and announcement-toned with precise amounts and eligibility windows?
13. **Right practice-area language?** Uses this area's actual scheme names and terms (TAC vs WorkCover vs TPD vs Fair Work vs AFCA), and the correct CTA model for the area (Free Claim Check vs super claim check vs paid consult vs class-action page)?
14. **Australian English, no em dashes?** Spelling is AU and every em dash is replaced with a comma, colon or full stop?
